About me

Hi, I'm Brie

I'm a civil engineering and philosophy student at UC Berkeley. On the engineering side, I'm most interested in where structures meet the Earth: foundations, coastlines, bridges, and the systems that keep cities running. Philosophy keeps me focused on the people behind every project. A seawall or a bridge changes how a whole community lives, works, and gets home at night, and I believe good engineering starts with listening to the people who will live with what we build.

My experiences have each taught me something I'll bring to my work: break a messy problem into pieces, take safety seriously, and stay calm when everyone's waiting on you.

On my desk
Sitting in the center of my desk is a clear jar of sea glass. Every piece was once dismissed as broken and worthless, until the tide softened its edges and carried it to shore. I study civil engineering to understand forces like that, slow and patient, and to build things that can stand up to them.

Capstone project

Capstone · In progressPacifica, CA

Pacifica Coastal Erosion Decision Tool

Pacifica is losing its coastline fast, and the city has to choose between building a seawall, managed retreat, or doing nothing. I'm building a tool that shows when a seawall stops making financial sense compared with retreat, and turns that answer into a summary a city could use in a grant application.

It adds up the cost of each option year by year through 2100 under low and high sea-level rise, finds the year when acting becomes cheaper than waiting, and checks the results against the city's own 2018 study. An early check already lines up: the erosion rates I measured at Esplanade Avenue, up to about 9 feet a year, match the city's report of 90 feet of bluff loss in a decade.
